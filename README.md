# Front Wing MK2 – Aerodynamic Design

### Goal
To design and develop an efficient multi-element front wing for a racing car, focused on generating high downforce while maintaining good aerodynamic efficiency.

### Method
- Designed a multi-element front wing with optimized camber, twist and endplates in Fusion 360
- Performed detailed geometric analysis (span, chord, aspect ratio, and surface curvature)
- Calculated downforce and drag using aerodynamic models, taking into account wing geometry and ground effect
- Prepared the model for further CFD analysis

### Results
- **Aspect Ratio (AR)**: ≈ 4.5 – 5.5
- **Downforce-to-Drag Ratio (DF/D)**: approximately **6.5 – 7.8**
- Expected downforce at 80 m/s: **1100 – 1400 N** (≈ 112–143 kgf) depending on angle of attack and ride height
- Improved aerodynamic efficiency compared to previous iterations thanks to the multi-element design and refined endplates

### Next Steps
- Run full CFD simulation (AirShaper / SimScale / OpenFOAM)
- Optimize angle of attack and ground clearance
- Test different Gurney flap configurations
- Validate performance through wind tunnel or track testing

### Conclusion
Front Wing MK2 demonstrates clear progress in aerodynamic performance. The design achieves competitive downforce levels with reasonable drag, making it suitable for Formula Student or similar racing applications. This project showcases the ability to apply aerodynamic principles to create an effective and practical racing component.
